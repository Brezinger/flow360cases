1 | Real input signal and preprocessing

step4 CFD pressure record. Figures are calculated from actual CSV samples. The detailed example
is plot observer 3, CSV column observer_2_pressure, with the script’s 105∘ angle label. Results for
all five observers are also reproduced. No synthetic replacement signal, measurement calibration
or distance adjustment is applied.

2 | Hann window and Fourier transform

The retained signal has 𝑁 = 22968 samples. The current setting NPERSEG="All" uses one full-length
segment. A 50% overlap is configured, but a second segment cannot fit; consequently this is a
single windowed periodogram evaluated through Welch’s interface. Shorter segments would allow
averaged periodograms, trading frequency resolution for statistical averaging.

The periodic Hann window and windowed discrete Fourier transform (DFT) are
                                                                       𝑁−1
                               𝑤𝑛 = 21 [1 − cos(2𝜋𝑛/𝑁 )] ,       𝑋𝑘 = ∑ 𝑤𝑛 𝑝𝑛′ exp(−i2𝜋𝑘𝑛/𝑁 ).
                                                                       𝑛=0

Mean removal precedes windowing. SciPy also applies its default constant detrend; for this one
segment it only removes any residual numerical mean. The record duration for the DFT is 𝑁 /𝑓𝑠 =
0.172261 s. This differs by one sample interval from the plotted first-to-last time span, (𝑁 − 1)/𝑓𝑠 .

Frequency bins are 𝑓𝑘 = 𝑘𝑓𝑠 /𝑁 and Δ𝑓 = 𝑓𝑠 /𝑁 = 5.805160 Hz. There is no FFT zero padding. For a
real pressure record, the negative-frequency power is folded into positive frequencies. Let 𝑐𝑘 = 2
for positive interior bins and 𝑐𝑘 = 1 for DC and the Nyquist bin (when present). The two SciPy
normalizations are [1]

                                            𝑐𝑘 |𝑋𝑘 |2      2                  𝑐𝑘 |𝑋𝑘 |2       2
                                   𝐺𝑘 =                 [Pa /Hz],     𝑆𝑘 =                 [Pa ].
                                           𝑓𝑠 ∑𝑛 𝑤𝑛2                         (∑𝑛 𝑤𝑛 )2

𝐺𝑘 is power spectral density (PSD); 𝑆𝑘 is the amplitude-normalized squared spectrum. The window
reduces boundary leakage but spreads tones across neighbouring bins. It also changes the time
weighting of a transient record. Window normalization compensates its power gain; it does not
restore information suppressed at the edges.

       does not guarantee the same accuracy for an off-bin tone or overlapping components. Integrating
       a suitable tone band is generally more robust.

5 | Erroneous and corrected calculations

The original spl_spectra_calculator.py and the former plotting script used the amplitude-
normalized 𝑆𝑘 both for the narrowband display and for summed levels. The display interpretation
was valid, but the direct power summation was not window-energy normalized:

                             𝑃wrong,𝑚 = ∑ 𝑆𝑘 ,         𝐿wrong,𝑚 = 10 log10 (𝑃wrong,𝑚 /𝑝02 ).
                                         𝑘∈𝑚

The same error affected weighted and unweighted OASPL and integrated harmonic bands. A-
weighting does not remove it because the normalization factor is constant and multiplies every
bin.

The equivalent noise bandwidth is

                                                    ∑𝑛 𝑤𝑛2
                                        𝐵𝑒 = 𝑓 𝑠             ,     𝑆 𝑘 = 𝐺 𝑘 𝐵𝑒 .
                                                   (∑𝑛 𝑤𝑛 )2

For the periodic Hann window, ∑ 𝑤𝑛 = 𝑁 /2, ∑ 𝑤𝑛2 = 3𝑁 /8, so 𝐵𝑒 = 1.5Δ𝑓. In this real record
𝐵𝑒 = 8.707741 Hz, hence

                             𝑃wrong,𝑚
                                        = 1.5,      Δ𝐿 = 10 log10 (1.5) = 1.76091259 dB.
                             𝑃correct,𝑚

This ratio is exact for matching bins and processing settings, apart from floating-point roundoff. It
is frequency independent. Other errors, including band-edge leakage, limited temporal resolution
or nonstationarity, need separate assessment.


Code comparison for a frequency-band sum

# Former calculation: amplitude spectrum used as additive power
f, S = welch(p_prime, fs=fs, window="hann", nperseg=N,
             noverlap=N//2, scaling="spectrum")
P_wrong = S[band_mask].sum()

# Correct power calculation
f, G = welch(p_prime, fs=fs, window="hann", nperseg=N,
             noverlap=N//2, scaling="density")
df = f[1] - f[0]
P_correct = (G[band_mask] * df).sum()
L_correct = 10 * log10(P_correct / (20e-6)**2)


For A-weighted sums, use 𝐺𝑘 Δ𝑓 10𝐴(𝑓𝑘 )/10 inside the sum. Decibels must not be added di-
rectly. If combining already integrated band levels with identical coverage, their energy sum is
10 log10 (∑𝑚 10𝐿𝑚 /10 ).

The implemented helper _welch_mean_square_spectrum now returns 𝐺𝑘 Δ𝑓. Narrowband ampli-
tude plots and nearest-bin tone extraction use a separate scaling="spectrum" call. There is there-
fore no blanket subtraction from individual tone peaks.

7 | Power checks and reproducibility

A full-frequency Parseval check distinguishes a numerical normalization error from the intended
window weighting. For the one-segment, mean-removed signal,
                                         Nyquist
                                                          ∑𝑛 𝑤𝑛2 (𝑝𝑛′ )2
                                          ∑ 𝐺𝑘 Δ𝑓 =                      .
                                          𝑘=0
                                                           ∑𝑛 𝑤𝑛2

The frequency-domain result is 0.00145304 Pa2 , and the corresponding time-domain result is
0.00145304 Pa2 . They agree to numerical precision. This check includes DC and all frequencies up
to Nyquist; the 40–10 000 Hz OASPL intentionally excludes power outside that band.

The unwindowed fluctuation RMS is 0.038118 Pa; the full-band Hann-normalized RMS is 0.038119
Pa. Their small difference in this record is expected. Equality with unwindowed RMS is not guaran-
teed for a transient signal, even after correct spectral normalization.

CSV timestamps are approximately uniform. Their maximum step deviation from the mean is about
0.0253%, consistent with finite timestamp precision; the implemented calculation uses the mean
interval. The retained absolute interval is 1.130448–1.302701 s, with sample indices 501–23468
(zero-based CSV data rows). No resampling was performed.


Reproduction package

The accompanying package includes the original CSV and build_figures.py, LaTeX sources and cor-
porate assets, analysis_results.json, per-observer OASPL and per-band CSV tables, standalone
vector figures, source-script snapshots and poc2_observer3_processing.npz. The NPZ contains the
actual retained dimensional pressure, time, Hann weights, PSD, amplitude spectrum and additive
bin powers. Results are generated using Python 3.11.9, SciPy 1.16.3 and NumPy 2.4.4.
